New to AI

Nervous about AI? Good. Start here.

You don't need to understand how it works to get something useful out of it today. This page takes about ten minutes, needs no account here, and you can't break anything by trying.

The honest answers to what people actually worry about

What is it, really?

The AI apps you've heard of (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot) are chat assistants. You type or talk, and it writes back. Under the hood it's a very large model trained on text to predict a good next word, which turns out to be enough to draft, explain, summarize and plan.

In a normal chat it doesn't go off and do things by itself. It answers when you ask. It only acts in the world (sends email, edits files) if someone connects it to those tools, and the good apps ask you before each action.

Will it take over the world?

Not from your kitchen table. The worry is about where powerful AI goes over the next decades, and serious people disagree about it. Those are real questions, and the companies building these tools publish their safety work so you can judge for yourself.

What you can control is how you use it today: as a helper whose work you check, not an authority you obey.

Can I trust what it says?

Not blindly. It can state something wrong with total confidence, especially numbers, laws, prices and anything recent. That's why every prompt here tells it not to invent facts, and why we have one whose whole job is to make it check its own work.

Rule of thumb: the more it matters, the more you verify with the original source.

What happens to what I type?

It goes to the company that runs the app. ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini each have a setting that controls whether your chats can be used to train future models, and the defaults differ. Don't paste passwords, card numbers or other people's private details.

Is my data safe? walks through the setting in each app, with links to the companies' own pages.

Do I have to pay?

No. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot all have free versions, and everything in this toolbox works on them unless the page says otherwise. Paid plans mostly raise limits and add features. Try free first.

About five minutes

Your first win: pick one, copy it, paste it in

  1. Open any AI chat app on your phone or computer. If you don't have one, the free version of any of them is fine.
  2. Pick the one below that fits your day. Press Copy prompt.
  3. Paste it into the chat. Replace the gold [BLANKS] with your details. Send.
  4. Not quite right? Reply like you would to a person: "shorter", "less formal", "no fish". That's the whole skill.

At home

Plan a week of dinners from what you already have

List what's in your fridge and pantry and who's eating. Get five weeknight dinners that use it up, plus one short grocery list for the gaps.

Prompt
Plan five weeknight dinners for my household.

What we already have (include spices, sauces and condiments): [WHAT'S IN THE FRIDGE AND PANTRY]
Who's eating (ages, picky eaters, anything someone can't eat): [WHO'S EATING]
Time and rules (e.g. 30 minutes max, one slow-cooker night, no fish): [TIME AND RULES]

Rules:
- Use up what we have first, especially anything that spoils soon. Say which ingredients each dinner uses up.
- Only use ingredients I listed, plus basics most kitchens have (salt, pepper, oil, butter, flour, sugar). If a dinner needs a spice or sauce I didn't list, put it on the grocery list marked "skip if you have it".
- For each dinner: name, total time, the 4 to 6 steps, and a "make it kid-friendly" tweak if anyone listed is under 12.
- End with ONE grocery list grouped by store section, with quantities, and nothing on it I already have.
- Never claim a dish is safe for an allergy. If someone can't eat something, flag every dinner where it could hide (sauces, broths, seasonings) so I can check labels.

At work

Answer an email you've been putting off, in your own voice

Paste an email you need to answer plus a few words on what you want to say. Get back a reply that sounds like you, not a robot.

Prompt
Draft a reply to the email below.

What I want to say: [WHAT I WANT TO SAY]

Match how I write. Here are a few emails I've written, for tone and length only (don't reuse their content):
[PASTE 2 OR 3 EMAILS YOU WROTE]

Rules:
- Lead with the answer or the decision in the first sentence
- Keep it shorter than the email I'm replying to, unless I need to explain something
- No "I hope this email finds you well", no "please don't hesitate to reach out"
- If my notes leave something unclear (a date, a price, a yes or no), put it in square brackets instead of guessing

The email I'm replying to:
[PASTE THE EMAIL]

Three habits that make the difference

1

Give it the background

It only knows what you tell it. "Write an email" gets a generic email. "Write an email to a customer who's waited two weeks for a part, apologetic, under 100 words" gets one you can send.

2

Push back

The first answer is a draft. Saying "that's too stiff" or "you missed the deadline" isn't rude, it's how it works. Most good results take two or three rounds.

3

Check what matters

Names, numbers, dates, laws, anything going to a customer. Ask it "what in your answer should I double-check?" and then check it.
