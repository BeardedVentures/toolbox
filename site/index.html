Answer an email you've been putting off, in your own voice

Paste an email you need to answer plus a few words on what you want to say. Get back a reply that sounds like you, not a robot.

PromptCopy & open Claude (opens in a new tab)
Draft a reply to the email below.

What I want to say: [WHAT I WANT TO SAY]

Match how I write. Here are a few emails I've written, for tone and length only (don't reuse their content):
[PASTE 2 OR 3 EMAILS YOU WROTE]

Rules:
- Lead with the answer or the decision in the first sentence
- Keep it shorter than the email I'm replying to, unless I need to explain something
- No "I hope this email finds you well", no "please don't hesitate to reach out"
- If my notes leave something unclear (a date, a price, a yes or no), put it in square brackets instead of guessing

The email I'm replying to:
[PASTE THE EMAIL]

Learn something new in a 20-minute lesson

Turns the AI into a patient tutor that teaches one idea at a time, checks you understood, and adapts to what you already know.

Copy this prompt into your AI, then replace the gold blanks. Or open the full page to fill it in here.

PromptCopy & open Claude (opens in a new tab)
Teach me [TOPIC]. I want to learn it because [WHY YOU WANT TO LEARN IT].

How to teach me:
- First, ask me two quick questions to find out what I already know.
- Then teach one idea at a time, in plain language, with an everyday example.
- After each idea, give me one quick question to check I got it. Wait for my answer.
- If I get it wrong, explain it a different way. Don't just repeat yourself.
- Keep each turn short. Plan about 5 ideas (roughly 20 minutes) and tell me which number we're on.
- After the fifth idea, or when I say "wrap up", give me a one-paragraph summary and the three things worth learning next.

Talk through a hard decision and get a straight answer

Lay out a decision you're stuck on. Get options, trade-offs, the question that actually decides it, and a straight recommendation.

Copy this prompt into your AI, then replace the gold blanks. Or open the full page to fill it in here.

PromptCopy & open Claude (opens in a new tab)
I need to decide: [THE DECISION]

What matters most to me: [WHAT MATTERS MOST TO ME]
Constraints (budget, time, people): [ANY CONSTRAINTS]

Do this:
1. List the realistic options (including "do nothing" and "wait") — no more than 4
2. For each: the upside, the downside, and what it would cost me in money, time and stress. If you estimate a number I didn't give you, mark it "(estimate)"
3. Name the one question that actually decides this
4. Give me your recommendation in one sentence, and what would change your mind
5. Suggest a small, cheap way to test it before fully committing, if there is one

Don't hedge. If you'd need more information to recommend, ask me for it first.

Turn messy meeting notes into who's doing what by when

Paste rough meeting notes or a call transcript. Get decisions, owners, deadlines and a follow-up email, cleanly separated.

Copy this prompt into your AI, then replace the gold blanks. Or open the full page to fill it in here.

PromptCopy & open Claude (opens in a new tab)
Below are rough notes (or a transcript) from a meeting. Turn them into:

1. **Decisions made** — one line each
2. **Action items** — a table with: task, owner, due date. If the notes don't say who or when, write "unassigned" or "no date" rather than guessing.
3. **Open questions** — anything raised but not settled
4. **Follow-up email** — a short recap I can send to everyone who attended, under 150 words

Don't add anything that isn't in the notes. If something is ambiguous, flag it at the end under "Check these".

Notes:
[PASTE NOTES OR TRANSCRIPT]

Follow up on a quote that went quiet

Three short follow-ups, a week apart, for a quote nobody answered. Easy to reply to, no guilt, no fake deadlines, and a polite last one so you can stop wondering.

Copy this prompt into your AI, then replace the gold blanks. Or open the full page to fill it in here.

PromptCopy & open Claude (opens in a new tab)
Write follow-ups for a quote I sent that hasn't had a reply.

What I quoted, to whom, and when: [WHAT I QUOTED AND WHEN]
What I know about them (what they cared about, anything they said): [WHAT I KNOW ABOUT THEM]
How I'll send it (text or email): [HOW I'LL SEND IT]

Write three messages I can send in order, about a week apart:
1. A short check-in that makes it easy to answer: one question they can reply to in a word.
2. One that adds something useful (a thing they cared about, an option that changes the price or timing, or an answer to a likely worry). Only use facts from my notes; if my notes don't give you one, write [ADD ONE USEFUL DETAIL] instead of inventing it.
3. A polite close-out that says I'll stop following up and the door stays open.

Rules:
- Text: under 300 characters each. Email: under 90 words, with a subject line.
- No guilt, no fake deadlines or "prices going up" unless my notes say so, no "just checking in" or "touching base".
- Use their first name only.

Then tell me in one line which message to skip if they've already said they're comparing quotes.

Set up an AI assistant that knows your business

Answer a few questions and get the full instruction set for a Claude Project, Custom GPT or Gemini Skill that works the way your business does.

Copy this prompt into your AI, then replace the gold blanks. Or open the full page to fill it in here.

PromptCopy & open Claude (opens in a new tab)
Help me build a custom AI assistant for: [WHAT THE ASSISTANT IS FOR]

Interview me first, one question at a time, to learn:
- Who will use it and what they'll ask it most
- What it should always do, and never do
- The tone it should use
- What documents or facts it should rely on
- What "a great answer" looks like, with an example if I have one

Then write the complete instructions I can paste into a Claude Project, a Custom GPT or a Gemini Skill. Keep them under 1,000 words so they fit a Custom GPT. Structure them with these headings: Role, Who you're helping, What you do, How to answer, Never do, When you don't know.

Finish with a list of the files I should upload alongside it, and 5 test questions I should ask it to make sure it works. Make at least 2 of them tricky: one it should refuse under "Never do" and one it should answer with "I don't know".

How we keep it honest

Nothing goes up untested.

Or if it does, it says so in plain words. Read the checklist every item has to pass.

  1. 1

    Run it for real

    Every prompt and skill is run with a realistic example. The page shows what we put in and what came back, trimmed.

  2. 2

    Write down where it fails

    Each page lists where it falls short. If testing found a problem, we fixed the prompt and ran it again.

  3. 3

    No made-up numbers

    No "saves 10 hours a week". Facts about apps and plans link to the company's own page, with the date we checked.
