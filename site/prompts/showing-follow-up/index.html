Prompt 01-21

Follow up after a showing or open house

Paste your quick notes on each visitor. Get a short, personal follow-up for each one that mentions what they actually said, plus who to call first.

Beginner Tested in ClaudeSolo operator
Prompt 
Write follow-up messages after a showing or open house.

Me and the property: [YOUR NAME AND THE PROPERTY]
My notes on each visitor (name, what they said, how interested they seemed, text or email). Use first names or initials, and don't paste phone numbers:
[NOTES ON EACH VISITOR]

For each visitor:
- If they asked a question my notes don't answer, say I'll find out and get back to them. Never answer it yourself.
- If my notes say they already have an agent, don't write to them. Write a short note I can send to their agent instead.
- If my notes give no text or email for someone, skip them and say so.
- Write the message in the channel I noted (text: under 300 characters; email: under 90 words with a subject line).
- Mention one specific thing they said or asked about, so it can't read as a mass message.
- End with one easy next step that fits their interest level (a second look, the disclosures, a list of similar homes, or simply "no pressure, here if you need anything").
- No pressure tactics, no fake urgency ("this won't last!"), no exclamation marks.

Then give me a short "Call first" list ranking who seemed most interested, with the reason from my notes.

Fill in the blanks

Type here and the prompt above fills itself in. Then copy it. Nothing you type leaves this page or gets saved.

2 blanks to go

Paste your own words, not client records, passwords or anything confidential. What you paste goes to the AI company.

What it actually does

Tested in Claude · Nov 1, 2026

Run by Claude while building this page. The example below is from that run, trimmed.

We filled in
YOUR NAME AND THE PROPERTY: Dana Reyes, open house Saturday at 412 Maple Ct, 4 bed 2.5 bath two-story, all bedrooms upstairs. NOTES ON EACH VISITOR: Marcus + Lee - loved backyard, asked if back fence is shared with neighbor, pre-approved, very interested, text. Ruth K. - downsizing, asked about the stairs, lukewarm, email. Anita - asked about HOA fees, interested, already has an agent, email. Tom - neighbor, just curious, no contact noted.
It came back with (trimmed)
Marcus + Lee (text, 240 characters) Hi Marcus and Lee, it's Dana from Saturday's open house at 412 Maple Ct. You asked whether the back fence is shared with the neighbor. I'm checking and will get back to you. Happy to set up a second look at the yard this week if you'd like. Ruth K. (email, 62 words) Subject: Your question about the stairs at 412 Maple Ct Hi Ruth, … You asked about the stairs: all four bedrooms are on the second floor. Since you mentioned downsizing, I'd be glad to send a short list of similar homes nearby … No pressure either way, I'm here if you need anything. … Anita has an agent, so this is for her agent (email, 51 words) Subject: HOA fees at 412 Maple Ct Hi [agent's name], Your client Anita … asked about the HOA fees. I'm confirming the current amount and will send it to you … Tom: no text or email in your notes, so I didn't write one. Call first 1. Marcus + Lee - pre-approved, very interested, waiting on the fence answer. 2. Anita (through her agent) - interested, HOA question open. 3. Ruth - lukewarm; the stairs may rule it out.

Where it falls short

  • Dana's notes don't name Anita's agent or give a contact, so the agent note has a '[agent's name]' placeholder and no address; Dana still has to find out who to send it to.
  • The 'Call first' list asks for a ranking of everyone, so a represented buyer still shows up as someone to call; the run had to add '(through her agent)' on its own to keep that straight.
  • The labels '240 characters', '62 words' and '51 words' are the model's own counts; they were right here (checked with a script), but the length caps rest on a model counting, which drifts on longer messages.

When to use it

Same evening, while you still remember who asked about the basement. Your notes can be rough: “Priya + Sam, loved kitchen, worried about street noise, text”.

Good to know

  • Only message people who gave you their contact details and agreed to hear from you. Text messages to people who didn’t opt in can break telemarketing rules.
  • Keep visitors’ notes out of anything shared; they’re for you.

Why it works

Generic “Thanks for stopping by!” follow-ups get ignored. Tying each one to a detail the visitor said, and matching the ask to their interest, is the part that gets replies.
