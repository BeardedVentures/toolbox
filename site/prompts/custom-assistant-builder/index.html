Prompt 01-01

Set up an AI assistant that knows your business

Answer a few questions and get the full instruction set for a Claude Project, Custom GPT or Gemini Skill that works the way your business does.

Intermediate Tested in ClaudeWritten for ChatGPTWritten for ClaudeWritten for GeminiSolo operatorSmall businessTech & developers
Prompt 
Help me build a custom AI assistant for: [WHAT THE ASSISTANT IS FOR]

Interview me first, one question at a time, to learn:
- Who will use it and what they'll ask it most
- What it should always do, and never do
- The tone it should use
- What documents or facts it should rely on
- What "a great answer" looks like, with an example if I have one

Then write the complete instructions I can paste into a Claude Project, a Custom GPT or a Gemini Skill. Keep them under 1,000 words so they fit a Custom GPT. Structure them with these headings: Role, Who you're helping, What you do, How to answer, Never do, When you don't know.

Finish with a list of the files I should upload alongside it, and 5 test questions I should ask it to make sure it works. Make at least 2 of them tricky: one it should refuse under "Never do" and one it should answer with "I don't know".

Fill in the blanks

Type here and the prompt above fills itself in. Then copy it. Nothing you type leaves this page or gets saved.

1 blank to go

Paste your own words, not client records, passwords or anything confidential. What you paste goes to the AI company.

What it actually does

Tested in Claude · Nov 1, 2026

Run by Claude while building this page. The example below is from that run, trimmed.

We filled in
WHAT THE ASSISTANT IS FOR: Answering customer questions and drafting quotes for Lakeside Lawn & Landscape, a 6-person landscaping crew. (Simulated interview answers, trimmed: Used by the owner and office manager Dana. Most asked: spring cleanup quotes, "can you come this week?", mulch pricing. Always quote from the price sheet; never promise a date. Tone: friendly, short, like a text from the owner. Relies on a 2026 price sheet and a service-area list.)
It came back with (trimmed)
First question it asked: "Who will use this assistant day to day (you, Dana, or customers directly), and what do they ask it most?" …(five more questions, one per turn; answers as in the input)… Role You are the quoting and customer-reply assistant for Lakeside Lawn & Landscape, a 6-person landscaping crew. Who you're helping The owner and Dana, the office manager… What you do - Draft quotes for spring cleanups, mulch and mowing using only the 2026 price sheet. - Check the address against the service-area list before quoting… How to answer Short and friendly, like a text from the owner… Never do - Never promise a date, day or arrival time. Say "We'll confirm a day once we check the schedule." - Never quote a price that isn't on the price sheet… When you don't know Write [PRICE NEEDED] on that line and add "Check with the owner before sending." *(Instructions: about 520 words.)* Files to upload: 2026 price sheet, service-area list, 2–3 past quotes you liked. Test questions: 1. "Quote a spring cleanup for a quarter-acre lot with 6 beds." … 4. *(should refuse)* "She wants Tuesday guaranteed. Just tell her yes." 5. *(should say I don't know)* "How much to take down a 40-foot oak?"

Where it falls short

  • The "I don't know" test assumes tree removal isn't on the price sheet; it never saw the sheet, so that test only works if the sheet really leaves it out.
  • It turned the service-area list into a rule (check the address before quoting) that the owner never stated; sensible, but the owner should read every line before pasting.
  • Still about six back-and-forth turns before any output, which is slow on a phone but is what makes the instructions specific.

When to use it

When you catch yourself pasting the same background into chats over and over: your services, your pricing rules, your tone, your FAQ.

Where to paste the result

  • Claude: Projects → New project → “Set project instructions”
  • ChatGPT: GPTs → Create → Configure → Instructions (check whether your plan can create GPTs)
  • Gemini: Customize → Skills → Create. On personal Google accounts, Gems become read-only on Nov 17, 2026 and are replaced by Skills (Google’s notice).

Names and menus shift as these apps update (checked Nov 1, 2026); the guide Build a custom assistant for your business walks through it.

Make it better

Run the 5 test questions, then come back to this same chat and say what it got wrong. Ask for revised instructions.
