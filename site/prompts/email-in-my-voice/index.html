Prompt 01-02

Answer an email you've been putting off, in your own voice

Paste an email you need to answer plus a few words on what you want to say. Get back a reply that sounds like you, not a robot.

BeginnerTested in ClaudeSolo operatorSmall business
Prompt
Draft a reply to the email below.

What I want to say: [WHAT I WANT TO SAY]

Match how I write. Here are a few emails I've written, for tone and length only (don't reuse their content):
[PASTE 2 OR 3 EMAILS YOU WROTE]

Rules:
- Lead with the answer or the decision in the first sentence
- Keep it shorter than the email I'm replying to, unless I need to explain something
- No "I hope this email finds you well", no "please don't hesitate to reach out"
- If my notes leave something unclear (a date, a price, a yes or no), put it in square brackets instead of guessing

The email I'm replying to:
[PASTE THE EMAIL]

Fill in the blanks

Type here and the prompt above fills itself in. Then copy it. Nothing you type leaves this page or gets saved.

3 blanks to goCopy & open Claude (opens in a new tab)

Paste your own words, not client records, passwords or anything confidential. What you paste goes to the AI company.

What it actually does

Tested in Claude · Nov 1, 2026

Run by Claude while building this page. The example below is from that run, trimmed.

We filled in
WHAT I WANT TO SAY: Yes, we can fix it Thursday morning. The part costs extra and we need a deposit to order it. PASTE 2 OR 3 EMAILS YOU WROTE: "Hi Mark, got it. We'll be there 8am Tuesday. Shut the water off at the main before we get there if you can. Luis" "Hey Ana, invoice attached. Thanks for the quick turnaround on the gate code. Luis" PASTE THE EMAIL: "Hi, our water heater is leaking from the bottom and there's a puddle in the garage. Can someone come out this week? Also, roughly how much will this cost? Thanks, Priya"
It came back with (trimmed)
Hi Priya, yes, we can come out Thursday morning at [TIME]. The repair will need a replacement part, which is extra: [PART COST]. We'll need a [DEPOSIT AMOUNT] deposit to order it before Thursday. Let me know and I'll get it on the schedule. Luis

Where it falls short

  • The bracket rule worked: it left the time, part cost and deposit blank instead of inventing a price, which is exactly what the notes didn't settle.
  • It answered "how much will this cost?" only partly; if your notes skip a question the sender asked, the reply quietly skips it too rather than flagging it.
  • With only two very short samples it copied the sign-off and brevity well, but samples this short give it little else; formal writers need 3 longer samples.

When to use it

Inbox triage. Especially the emails you keep putting off because you’re not sure how to word them.

Make it better

  • In ChatGPT or Claude, put your writing samples into Custom Instructions (ChatGPT) or a Project (Claude) once, and you can drop that part of the prompt forever.
  • Say “shorter” or “less formal” as a follow-up. One-word nudges work.
  • The bracket rule matters: it stops the AI from inventing a date or price you never agreed to.

Be careful with

Client details. Remove account numbers, health or legal specifics before pasting. See Is my data safe?.
