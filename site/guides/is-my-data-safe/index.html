Guide 03-02

Is my data safe? What to share and what not to

The plain-English rules for using AI with business information, where the training setting lives in Claude, Gemini and ChatGPT, and a simple "never paste" list.

5 min readBeginnerStart hereUpdated Nov 1, 2026

In Anthropic’s survey of 503 small-business decision-makers, data security was the most-cited barrier to using AI. It’s a fair worry. The good news: a few habits cover the biggest risks.

The short version

  1. Never paste passwords, full card or bank numbers, Social Security numbers, or login codes. No AI task needs them.
  2. Strip names and numbers from customer, patient or employee details before pasting. The AI can do the job with “Customer A”.
  3. Check the training setting in your app (below), and decide on purpose.
  4. Use a business plan if your team will use AI with client work. They come with stronger privacy terms.
  5. You’re responsible for the output. Read it before it goes to a customer.

The training setting

Consumer AI apps may use your conversations to improve their models, depending on a setting you control. Here’s what we could confirm on Nov 1, 2026:

  • Claude: your chats are used for training only if you allow it, under Model Improvement in Claude’s privacy settings. If you opt in, that data can be kept for up to 5 years, de-identified.
  • Gemini: the setting is Keep Activity (Gemini Apps Activity). While it’s on, a subset of chats is reviewed by people. When it’s off, chats are still kept for up to 72 hours.
  • ChatGPT: look in Settings → Data Controls for a setting about improving the model. We couldn’t reach OpenAI’s help center to confirm the current wording or default, so check it yourself.

Team and business plans from the major providers generally don’t train on your data by default. Check the plan’s terms before you rely on that.

A simple traffic-light rule

Green: go ahead Yellow: anonymize first Red: don’t paste
Your own drafts, marketing copy, public info, generic questions Customer emails, contracts, financials, employee issues Passwords, card/bank/SSN numbers, medical records, anything under an NDA or legal hold

Connectors and MCP tools

When you connect AI to your email, Drive or files, it can read what you give it access to. Every tool in our MCP library lists exactly what it can touch. Two good habits:

  • Start narrow. Give it one folder, not your whole drive.
  • Keep approvals on. In Claude, set any connector tool that sends, deletes or changes things to Needs approval.

If you’re in a regulated field

Healthcare, legal, finance and government work come with their own rules about where client data can go. Ask your compliance person or professional body before using AI with client records.

Where this comes from

  • Claude Privacy Center: model improvement and data retention
