What's an MCP, and why should I care?

A short, jargon-free explanation of MCP servers, the plug-ins that let AI assistants actually do things with your apps and files.

3 min readBeginnerStart hereUpdated Nov 1, 2026

The one-sentence answer

An MCP server is a small plug-in that lets an AI assistant use a tool, like your files, your calendar or a web page, instead of just talking about it.

An analogy

Think of your AI assistant as a smart new hire on day one. Without MCP, they can only work with what you hand them across the desk: you copy, they read, you paste the result somewhere.

MCP gives them their own keys to specific rooms. “Here’s access to the shared drive. Here’s the calendar.” Now you can say “find last month’s proposal and update the pricing” and they go get it.

MCP stands for Model Context Protocol. It’s an open standard, which means one plug-in can work across different AI apps. The MCP project itself compares it to a USB-C port: one shape of plug that fits lots of devices.

Two ways to get them

  1. Built-in connectors. The easy way. In Claude (and increasingly other assistants) you click “connect”, sign in to Google Drive or another app, and you’re done. Directory connectors work on every Claude plan, Free included. See how.
  2. Install one yourself. For tools that aren’t built in, like letting Claude Desktop work with a folder on your computer. It’s a copy-and-paste job in a settings file. Here’s the walkthrough.

What to watch

The power is the point, and also the risk. A tool that can read your email can read whatever you let it reach when you sign in, which may be all of your email. That’s why every tool in our library says, up front, exactly what it can touch.

Start narrow. In Claude, each connector’s tools can be set to Always allow, Needs approval or Blocked; set anything that sends, changes or deletes to Needs approval.

What we build

If there’s an app you wish your AI could reach (your CRM, your scheduling software, your inventory sheet), tell us. Building MCP servers is what we do, and the popular requests go in the free library.
