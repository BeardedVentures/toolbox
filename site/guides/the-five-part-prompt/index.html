Guide 03-04

Get better answers by telling AI these five things

The simple structure behind every prompt in our library, and why one-line requests come back bland. Learn it once and use it in any assistant.

6 min readBeginnerGet more out of itUpdated Nov 1, 2026

“Write me a marketing email” gets you something bland. Not because the AI is bad, but because it’s guessing. Give it five things and it stops guessing.

The five parts

  1. Role and audience. Who’s speaking, and to whom? “You’re writing as the owner of a family bakery, to regular customers.”
  2. The job. What you want, specifically. “An email announcing our new Saturday hours.”
  3. Context. What it can’t know. “We’re opening at 6am instead of 8 because of requests from early commuters. Starts Nov 9.”
  4. Rules. Length, tone, what to avoid. “Under 120 words. Friendly, not salesy. No exclamation marks in the subject line.”
  5. Format. What the output should look like. “Give me 3 subject lines and the body.”

Before and after

Before: Write an email about our new hours.

After: You’re writing as the owner of a family bakery, to regular customers. Write an email announcing our new Saturday hours. We’re opening at 6am instead of 8 because early commuters asked; it starts Nov 9. Under 120 words, friendly, not salesy. Give me 3 subject lines and the body.

Same request, but now the AI isn’t guessing at the audience, the reason, the length or the format.

Three upgrades

  • Show an example. “Here’s an email I liked, match this style” beats any description of a style. Anthropic’s own prompting guide recommends examples for the same reason.
  • Let it ask. End with “Ask me anything you need before you start.” Or use the interview me first prompt.
  • Mark your blanks. In saved prompts, put the parts that change in [BRACKETS]. That’s why our library highlights them.

Don’t overdo it

You don’t need all five parts for every question. “What’s 18% of $2,340?” is fine as is. Use the full structure when the answer matters or will be seen by someone else.

Where this comes from
